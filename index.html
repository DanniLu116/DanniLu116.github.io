About

I use data science, machine learning, and systems thinking to turn complex, multidisciplinary data into actionable insights for real-world decision-making.

My work focuses on problems at the intersection of transportation, sustainability, urban systems, and public health. I have developed machine-learning approaches for spatiotemporal PM2.5 hotspot detection, modeled transportation–environment–health interactions in New York City, and evaluated transportation policies such as congestion pricing.

I am currently pursuing a Ph.D. in Systems Engineering at Cornell University, where my research combines geospatial analytics, system dynamics, and integrated systems modeling to understand complex urban challenges and evaluate potential interventions.

My technical experience includes Python, R, SQL/MySQL, ArcGIS, machine learning, geospatial analysis, system dynamics, transportation modeling, and environmental modeling. I especially enjoy bridging technical analysis with practical decision-making and communicating complex results in a clear and useful way.

I am interested in applying data science and systems approaches to real-world challenges in mobility, sustainability, urban systems, environmental analytics, and health, and I am always happy to connect with people working across these areas.

Research Interests

Traffic Emissions & Air Quality

On-road and portable-emission measurements, carbon and NOx emission factors, and pollutant dispersion in urban street canyons.

Spatiotemporal ML

Machine learning for PM2.5 hotspot detection and targeted mitigation in cities.

Congestion Pricing

Evaluating New York City's cordon pricing and modeling long-run toll scenarios with system dynamics.

Environment & Health

Links between urban infrastructure, neighborhood deprivation, and health outcomes such as EGFR-mutant lung cancer.
